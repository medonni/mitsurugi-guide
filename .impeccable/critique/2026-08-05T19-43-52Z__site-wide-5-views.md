## Overall Impression

The visual system is genuinely good and the interaction layer is dragging it down by roughly a full band. Nothing here reads as machine-generated: the card row is real information design, the vocabulary is authored by someone with reps, and the restraint is deliberate rather than timid. Then you click something and the numbers stop matching reality, one control silently deletes content it claims to have restored, and the page's stated job (look up one card) is the one thing it cannot do.

The single biggest opportunity is small and specific: **this repo already contains the correct implementation of its own worst component.** The matchups filter has multi-select, `aria-pressed`, live-recomputed counts, and a visible Clear. The compendium filter, on a denser and more-visited page, has single-select, no ARIA, stale counts, and a reset that appears only after failure. Promoting the better pattern into `components.njk` fixes most of Status, Consistency, Recognition, and Recovery in one change.

## What's Working

**The card row is the reason the site earns its existence.** `components.njk:107-123` composes role label, name, type badges, material-cost pill, then two *named* columns: "WHERE IT ACTIVATES" (colored zone pills with plain-language effects) and "TIPS & TRICKS" (gold-headed, diamond bullets). It answers where and how-to-play in one glance, which is verbatim the stated primary job, and because it is a shared macro every deck inherits it identically. A wiki structurally cannot do this.

**The domain vocabulary is authored, and it is what defeats the slop read.** Step "1.5" in a timeline. "the payoff" as a group tag. Four annotation tags that mean genuinely different things. Copy like "The reveal is once per Duel, so an Ash Blossom on it can end your game." No card database produces that sentence.

**WIP is treated as content rather than absence.** The matchups page leads with "10 interactions mapped so far" before you can be disappointed. The 2-Card section ships an explanatory panel instead of a gap, and links to the lines that already cover the payoff. Sacred Beasts carries its badge in the nav, on the hub card, and beside its `h1`. Setting expectations three times converts a gap into a credibility signal.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] "Clear ✕" on the matchups page permanently destroys 6 of 8 matchups
**Why it matters.** Deterministic, and I verified the mechanism in the source. On load, all 8 `.hit-deck-block`s show. Click any handtrap chip and the 6 "not mapped yet" stubs go `hidden`. Click Clear and every chip returns to `aria-pressed="false"`, the button hides itself, and Kewl Tunes, Doomz, Elfnote, Maliss, Light and Darkness Ritual and Blitzclique **stay hidden**, along with their JUMP TO links. The UI now states no filter is active while showing a filtered page. Only a reload recovers.

The cause is `matchups.njk:102-104`: `const shown = b.querySelector(".hit-row:not([hidden])"); b.hidden = !shown;`. A stub block has zero `.hit-row` children, so `shown` is `null` and `b.hidden` becomes `true` regardless of whether any filter is active. The comment six lines above shows the author handled exactly this case in `recount()` and missed it here.

Those stubs are how a reader learns the page *knows about* their matchup and has not gotten to it yet. Deleting them turns "not written up yet" into "your deck isn't here", and the control that did it claims to have undone itself.

**Fix.** One line: `b.hidden = sel.size !== 0 && !shown;`
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] The compendium cannot find a card by name, on a site whose stated job is looking up one card
**Why it matters.** There are zero `input` elements on `/mitsurugi/cards/`. Finding "Ame no Habakiri" means scrolling 30 rows at roughly 700px each on mobile, or guessing which of 7 section tabs holds it. Ctrl+F is not a fallback: once a filter is applied, non-matching rows are `display:none` and browser find skips them. Both existing axes (zone, section) require the reader to already know the answer to the question they are asking. PRODUCT.md's opening line about the audience is that they arrive mid-research looking up one card, and the name is the one axis the page cannot filter on.

**Fix.** One text input above the chips, filtering `.card-row` on its `h3` text, folded into the existing `apply()` in `card-filter.js` as a third state key. Roughly ten lines. It must auto-open any collapsed `<details>` containing a match.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable craft` (or `/impeccable harden` if you want it minimal)

### [P1] Filter counts lie, and the collapsed-group case fails silently
**Why it matters.** Three linked failures in `card-filter.js`. The `.count` spans are never touched, so with ON TRIBUTE active the tabs still read "All Cards 30 / Spell / Traps 5" for groups with zero matches; clicking "Spell / Traps 5" delivers nothing. `groupHead`'s baked count has the same problem. Worst, collapse a group and then filter: `anyGroup` stays true because rows inside a closed `<details>` still pass the `:not([style*="none"])` test, so the empty-state message never fires. The reader sees a collapsed header claiming "3", zero cards, and the synergy panel below (which is never filtered) reading as though those are the results.

The counts are the only quantitative signal on the page and the only thing telling a reader whether a filter is worth clicking. Wrong under exactly the conditions they are needed is worse than absent.

**Fix.** Recompute `.tab .count` and `.group-count` from visible rows inside `apply()`; the working pattern is `recount()` at `matchups.njk:96-100`. Change the empty check from "no group survived" to "no row is visible", and hide the synergy block when the compendium is empty.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] Reading text runs to 97-110 characters per line on the three densest pages
**Why it matters.** This is the finding both LLM assessments missed and the detector caught. I measured it directly with canvas text metrics at 1280px:

| Element | Size | Measured |
|---|---|---|
| `.combo-do` (combo step instructions) | 14.5px | **110ch** |
| `.hit-note` (matchup timing notes) | 13px | **108ch** |
| `.hit-targets` (what a handtrap hits) | 14.5px | **97ch** |
| `.group-blurb` (compendium group intro) | 14.5px | **80ch** |

Against a 65-75ch cap. Every element that *has* an explicit `max-width` measures fine (`.intro` 67ch, `.hero p` 53ch, `.prose p` 59ch, `.zone-text` 43ch), so this is not a typographic blind spot: it is prose that was never given a width and inherited the container. The affected text is the primary reading content of the combos and matchups pages, at the smallest sizes on the site, which compounds the return-sweep cost.

**Fix.** `max-width: 68ch` on `.combo-do`, `.hit-targets`, `.hit-note`, and `.group-blurb`. Four declarations.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable typeset`

### [P2] The boss stack's rest state renders a Yu-Gi-Oh card that does not exist
**Why it matters.** At ≥901px the three bosses are diagonal `clip-path` slices of one card-sized frame at `brightness(0.8)`. Because every YGO card frame has identical geometry, the composite does not read as a triptych, it reads as one legible card. Independently confirmed twice: A captured a title bar reading **"Ame no Murakuri no Mitsurugi"** (a splice naming no real card) over a spliced effect box and a stat line of `ATK/2000 DEF/3400` belonging to no card in the deck, and I had seen the same chimera in an earlier screenshot without recognizing what it was. The detector reached the same element from a third direction via `clipped-overflow-container`.

This is the hero of the flagship deck page and the first thing a desktop reader sees. This audience reads names and stat lines with legal precision, and the site's entire pitch is accurate simplification. It resolves correctly on hover, but only for a mouse user who happens to hover.

**Fix.** Make the rest state unmistakably not-a-card: add `filter: blur(1.5px) brightness(0.55)` to `.boss-slice` and drop it on `:hover` alongside the existing `filter: none`. The transition is already declared. Alternatively offset each slice's `background-position` vertically so the title bars stop aligning.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

## Minor Observations

- **Cognitive load fails 4 of 8.** Grouping (the 390px group header crams a 2-line title, a 2-line tag, a count and a chevron into ~110px, all baseline-mismatched). One-thing-at-a-time (two filter axes with no stated relationship). Minimal choices, three times over: compendium 5 chips + 7 tabs = 12, matchups 6 chips + 9 jumps = 15, mobile nav sheet 18 links pre-expanded at 656px tall on an 844px viewport. Working memory (no search, no "showing 3 of 30", and counts the reader must actively distrust).
- **Both deep pages end on a to-do list.** The compendium closes with "More deck variants in progress: Ryzeal · Sky Striker · Clown Crew · Chaos"; combos closes with "More lines to document: Fiendsmith bridge". The WIP framing at the *top* of these pages is exemplary; the same honesty at the bottom reads as an apology. Peak-end says the last thing a reader carries away is "this isn't finished."
- **DESIGN.md §3 documents a banned pattern the code correctly avoids**: "On the payoff word it takes a gold→violet clip gradient". There is no `background-clip` anywhere. The doc is an open invitation for someone to "restore" gradient text.
- **`a.gold` (`style.css:237`) puts links on gold**, placing gold on the interaction side of the Two Voices Rule. Sacred Beasts' intro has four gold links in six lines, roughly 40% of the paragraph, which also breaks the Scarce Gold Rule. Mitsurugi's equivalent has two and reads fine. The rule is right; the application is uneven. The Mitsurugi spec strip likewise spends gold on three of four values.
- The mobile nav sheet at `rgba(20,16,31,0.98)` lets the 44px headline ghost through legibly. Take it to `1`.
- The white 2px selection ring lands on ON TRIBUTE (gold fill) and TRIGGER (violet fill), the two lightest chips, where white-on-light is the weakest pairing.
- The boss-slice overlay prints `LV8 · ATK 2400 · DEF 1800` two lines below the card's own printed stat line, covering the art to do it.
- Hub heading order is `h1 → h3 → h3 → h3 → h2` (`index.njk:21` before `:32`).
- Filter state is not in the URL on either filtered page, so a filtered view cannot be shared or survive a refresh. `<details>` state does not persist across navigation, and the compendium has no expand/collapse-all though the combos page has one.
- `card-filter.js:29` detects visible rows with `'.card-row:not([style*="none"])'`, a substring match on the style attribute. Works today, breaks silently the first time any inline style contains "none".
- Contrast measured clean throughout: worst case 4.98:1 on 10-12px mono labels, which passes AA.

## Questions to Consider

1. **The compendium and the matchups page solve the same problem, and the better solution lost.** Matchups has multi-select, `aria-pressed`, live counts, and a visible Clear. The compendium, denser and more visited, has none of them. Same author, same site. What made the second implementation not inherit the first, and which one belongs in `components.njk`?
2. **If a reader knows the card name, the compendium cannot help them.** "Organize by function" is the right *display* principle. Is it being allowed to also dictate the *access* method, when those are separable decisions?
3. **The signature costs correctness to get.** Is a wow moment that requires the flagship page to display a nonexistent card for the 95% of the time nobody is hovering worth keeping unmodified, when a 1.5px blur buys the same reveal for free?
4. **Both deep pages end on what is missing.** What would each close with if it ended on what the reader just gained instead of what you have not written yet?
5. **The thirty tips on the compendium carry almost no cross-links** while the combos page links nearly every card name. Is that an authoring backlog, or an unwritten rule against linking non-archetype cards?
